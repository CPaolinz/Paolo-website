# Research and editorial source notes

This project is a first-person personal-site concept drafted from public records and selected publications. The first-person wording is proposed copy, not an authenticated statement by Paolo Paolini; it needs his review and approval before public release. Collaborative HOC-LAB work is explicitly distinguished from papers I co-authored, and the site is not a complete CV.

## Institutional profile and research context

- **Politecnico di Milano, DEIB profile** — https://www.deib.polimi.it/eng/people/details/60294
  - Lists Paolo Paolini as a retired professor in Computer Science and Engineering and identifies the research line as “Data, web, and society.”
  - Describes work on multimedia-application requirements and design, collaborative e-learning, technology-mediated communication, and cultural-heritage applications.
  - Reports more than 70 papers on this profile; other contexts report different counts, so the website avoids a personal publication total.
- **HOC-LAB official research page** — https://www.hoc.polimi.it/en/research/
  - Describes work on concept-centred augmented reality, EVOLI video annotation, educational chatbots, multimedia/interactive digital stories, and technology-supported education.
  - States that the work is investigated through design principles and impact, favoring experimentation in real contexts.
- **HOC-LAB official About page** — https://www.hoc.polimi.it/en/about/
  - Describes the lab as a multidisciplinary multimedia communication and innovative-education lab founded in 1997.
  - Provides context on digital storytelling, EVOLI, TalkyTutor, cultural-heritage storytelling, and a Horizon 2020 societal project on cognitive health / ageing, including six pilot cities in Europe and Singapore.

## Editorial interpretation versus facts

The phrases “I work in the spaces between information and understanding,” “I started with physics. I kept returning to people,” and “I care about what a connection makes possible” are editorial framings proposed for this concept, not quotations from published work. They should be reviewed by Paolo Paolini before this concept is used as his personal website.

The chronology selects a few papers and research directions to communicate a line of inquiry. It is not a full career timeline or complete CV. The first-person degree and appointment claims are based on the CV text supplied for this project and institutional profile context; the final wording should be checked against an authorized CV and approved by the site owner.
## Field section attribution

The “Where my work meets the world” cards describe collaborative HOC-LAB research themes, not individual-only projects. Cultural-heritage storytelling is linked to the 2014 chapter “Storytelling for Cultural Heritage”; collaborative learning is linked to the 2014 Di Blas and Paolini study. The 9,000+ figure applies to four programmes in that study (2002–2009), not to Paolini personally. The accessibility wording is supported by HOC-LAB publications on educational cultural-heritage websites for blind users; the ageing-related wording describes the aim of a past European project, not a claim that its effectiveness has been established.

## Reading Room map methodology

The Reading Room illustration is an editorial map of selected works, not a model proposed by Paolini and not a chronology. Solid links group concepts described together in the Hypertext Design Model (HDM)—information structure, link categories, and browsing semantics—supported by the 1991 and 1993 HDM publications listed above. Dashed links are editorial associations between separate selected works on consistent database views (1988), cultural-heritage storytelling (2014), and collaborative learning (2014). They do not claim that those publications form one project or that the dashed relationships were asserted by their authors. A visible legend on the page explains this distinction.
